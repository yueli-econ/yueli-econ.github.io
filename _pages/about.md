I am a PhD student in Economics at University College London. I study education and human capital, with a focus on peer interactions and socio-emotional development.

Using field experiments in Chinese schools, my work on [Bedtime Stories, Daytime Effects](/research/#bedtime-stories-daytime-effects-classroom-spillovers-in-rural-china) examines whether a programme’s benefits extend beyond its direct recipients. My ongoing project, [Building Supportive Peer Networks](/research/#building-supportive-peer-networks-experimental-evidence-from-chinese-middle-schools), asks whether schools can help students form supportive peer relationships.

I also work on survey response scales and self-reported time use, and on the labour-market effects of higher education expansion.

I received my MPhil in Economics degree from St Cross College, University of Oxford (2021). Before that, I graduated from Nankai University with a BSc in Economics.

You can find the latest version of my **CV** [here](/files/Yue_Li_CV.pdf).
