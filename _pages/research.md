## Working Papers

### “Bedtime Stories, Daytime Effects: Classroom Spillovers in Rural China”  
*with [Peiyi Jin](https://peiyi-jin.github.io/)* 

Abstract: Can a school intervention benefit classmates outside its formal delivery group? We studied a randomised socio-emotional program in 63 rural Chinese primary schools. In treated schools, 15-minute audio bedtime stories were broadcast before bedtime in dormitories, targeting boarding students after day students had returned home. Both groups shared classrooms and teachers during the day. After 14 months, baseline day students in treated schools reported self-esteem 0.15 standard deviations higher and depressive symptoms 0.12 standard deviations lower than those in control schools. The self-esteem gain extends to children with no recorded reciprocal friendship with a boarding student at baseline. Day students also reported more story-related reading, more parental storytelling, and a better peer climate. Average treatment effects on non-cognitive skills among boarding students were small and imprecise, but estimated gains were larger in classrooms with lower baseline boarding shares. These findings suggest that a program’s effects can extend beyond its formal delivery group, and that evaluating formal recipients alone may understate its effects.

*Presented at: RES 2025 (Birmingham), Janeway Institute PhD Workshop 2025 (Cambridge), SOLE 2024 (Oregon), EAYE 2024 (Paris)*

## Primary Data Collection

### “Study and Development of Junior High Students”, China (2015–2020)
Panel dataset with four survey waves (2015, 2016, 2017, 2020) covering ~18,000 students in rural Sichuan and Hebei, along with their teachers and principals. Responsible for the 2020 follow-up wave, including leading survey implementation, cleaning ~20,000 student responses, and constructing non-cognitive skills measures.
Data includes: mental health, social network, buly behaviours, internet addiction, cognitive and non-cognitive skills.
